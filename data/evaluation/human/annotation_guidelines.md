# IndicLearn Human Annotation Guidelines (Phase 9)

## 1. Overview & Evaluator Role

You are evaluating answers, recommendations, and assessment items generated by the **IndicLearn** educational prototype in the domain of **Signals and Systems** engineering.

Your role is to provide rigorous, objective assessments. You will be evaluating student queries formulated across four linguistic varieties:
1. **English (`en`)**
2. **Telugu Script (`te`)**
3. **Romanized Telugu (`te-Latn`)**
4. **Telugu-English Code-Mix (`te-en`)**

---

## 2. Core Epistemic Principles

### Rule A: Factual Plausibility $\ne$ Grounded Support
> **Critical Instruction:** If the retrieved evidence provided with the query does **NOT** explicitly support a claim, you **MUST** mark the Evidence Support as low (1 or 2), even if the statement is factually true according to your general engineering knowledge. The system is evaluated on its ability to ground statements in student-uploaded course materials, not on ungrounded pre-trained memory.

### Rule B: Fluency $\ne$ Correctness
> A beautifully phrased, highly fluent answer can be completely wrong or subtly misleading. Grade `Clarity` and `Factual Correctness` independently. Do not let elegant phrasing inflate your correctness score.

### Rule C: Model Confidence $\ne$ Ground Truth
> The system's internal confidence estimate reflects evidence volume and model consistency, not cognitive certainty. **Do not infer correctness from the model's displayed confidence score.** Judge each item solely on the text and evidence provided.

---

## 3. Rating Rubric (1 to 5 Ordinal Scale)

For each query and model output, assign integer ratings from 1 to 5 across the following dimensions:

### Dimension 1: Factual Correctness
* **1 — Incorrect:** The core engineering concepts, equations, or explanations are factually erroneous or mathematically false.
* **2 — Mostly Incorrect:** Contains significant technical errors with only minor tangential factual points.
* **3 — Partially Correct:** Contains an accurate core intuition but suffers from noticeable inaccuracies, omitted conditions, or confused definitions.
* **4 — Mostly Correct:** Conceptually and mathematically sound; may have minor cosmetic omissions that do not compromise technical understanding.
* **5 — Fully Correct:** Completely accurate, rigorous, and faithful to standard undergraduate engineering textbooks.

### Dimension 2: Evidence Support (Grounding)
* **1 — Unsupported:** The answer makes claims that are entirely absent from or contradicted by the provided curriculum chunks.
* **2 — Weakly Supported:** Only a fraction of the substantive claims are supported; major assertions rely on external unsupplied knowledge.
* **3 — Partially Supported:** Approximately half the claims find support in the chunks; some extrapolation is present.
* **4 — Well Supported:** Nearly all substantive claims directly correspond to information in the supplied chunks.
* **5 — Fully Supported:** Every single substantive claim is directly and unambiguously supported by the cited curriculum chunks.

### Dimension 3: Citation Correctness
* **1 — Severe Hallucination:** Citations cite nonexistent chunks (`[S999]`), or cite chunks that discuss completely unrelated topics.
* **2 — Mostly Invalid:** Most citations do not support the specific sentences they are attached to.
* **3 — Partially Valid:** Citations are relevant to the topic, but sentence-level attribution is imprecise or misaligned.
* **4 — Mostly Valid:** Citations accurately attribute the relevant claims, with minor formatting or placement imperfections.
* **5 — Fully Valid:** Every citation points to a valid supplied chunk that contains the exact evidence for that sentence.

### Dimension 4: Pedagogical Usefulness
* **1 — Counter-Productive / Confusing:** Would confuse an engineering student or reinforce common misconceptions.
* **2 — Low Usefulness:** Too vague, overly terse, or fragmented to facilitate conceptual learning.
* **3 — Moderately Useful:** Provides acceptable basic explanation but lacks intuitive framing, prerequisite scaffolding, or worked context.
* **4 — Useful:** Explains the engineering concept clearly with good scaffolding for an undergraduate learner.
* **5 — Exemplary:** Exceptional pedagogical explanation that clarifies underlying principles, addresses potential confusion, and connects concepts effectively.

### Dimension 5: Clarity & Structure
* **1 — Incoherent:** Disorganized, rambling, fragmented, or difficult to parse.
* **2 — Poor Structure:** Heavy reading friction; awkward transitions.
* **3 — Adequate:** Understandable on careful reading; average structure.
* **4 — Clear:** Well-organized, logical flow, easy to read and understand.
* **5 — Crystal Clear:** Flawless organization, concise, intuitive, and engaging.

### Dimension 6: Language Quality & Script Naturalness
* **1 — Garbled / Unnatural:** Corrupted Unicode characters, broken syntax, or unreadable Telugu translation.
* **2 — Stilted / Machine-Translated:** Literal dictionary translations of technical terms that sound unnatural to Telugu engineering speakers (e.g. over-translating *"convolution"* or *"system"* into obscure archaisms).
* **3 — Acceptable:** Understandable phrasing; minor syntactic stiffness.
* **4 — Natural:** Idiomatic phrasing that mirrors real classroom discourse.
* **5 — Highly Fluent:** Fluent, natural academic phrasing respecting Telugu conventions.

### Dimension 7: Code-Mixed Understanding (For `te-en` and `te-Latn` queries)
*(Score N/A if query and answer are entirely in pure English)*
* **1 — Misunderstood:** The system failed to parse the English technical terms embedded in Telugu syntax or completely misinterpreted Romanized transliteration.
* **2 — Heavy Friction:** Misparsed key technical acronyms or treated Telugu grammatical particles as English words.
* **3 — Acceptable Bridging:** Correctly identified the technical terms despite phonetic or grammatical mixing.
* **4 — Good Bridging:** Natural handling of code-mixed student phrasing (e.g. *"LTI system properties cheppandi"*).
* **5 — Seamless:** Native-like interpretation of Telugu-English code-mixing, preserving English technical terms with natural Telugu grammatical scaffolding.

---

## 4. Disagreement & Adjudication Protocol

1. Annotations will be collected independently by at least two evaluators.
2. Annotators must **never** discuss specific ratings while an evaluation round is active.
3. Where ratings differ by 2 or more points on any 1–5 scale, an independent third evaluator will adjudicate.
4. **All raw individual ratings are permanently preserved** in evaluation datasets to enable accurate inter-rater reliability analysis ($\kappa$).
